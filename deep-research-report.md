## Integration notes and remaining gaps

For immediate production work, the most practical additions are DriveNC, OHGO, PennDOT’s ArcGIS layer, DGT DATEX, Norway DATEX CCTV, Hong Kong TD snapshots, Taiwan Civil IoT CCTV, and Panama Canal Authority pages. Those are the sources from this pass with the best combination of public legality, machine readability, explicit geometry, and visible reuse conditions. citeturn52view2turn53view0turn51view2turn30view0turn18view2turn19search0turn19search8turn35search1

The biggest unresolved gaps after this pass are Louisiana, Mississippi, New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, Wisconsin, Michigan, Virginia, South Carolina, most Canadian provinces outside the ones you already have, and much of Japan, Korea, Latin America, the Caribbean, and Africa. In several of those regions, official public camera pages clearly exist, but the surfaced evidence did not expose a bulk catalog, stable media URLs, or documented API schema. The next research pass should focus on hidden ArcGIS layers, vendor-backed 511 developer docs, and official transport open-data portals for those remaining gaps.

Operationally, I would split ingestion into three tiers. Tier one should be stable APIs or geospatial services. Tier two should be catalog pages where the schema is public but the media endpoint still needs a follow-up inspection. Tier three should be HTML source-page integrations with conservative rate limits, robots-aware crawling, and strong failure handling. That tiered approach fits the evidence from this research pass, where the strongest official sources are clean APIs in some regions and carefully structured public pages in others.
